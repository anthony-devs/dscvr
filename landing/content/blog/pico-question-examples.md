PICO is a way of turning a vague clinical question into one you can actually search for and answer. It's taught in medicine, nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy and public health because a well-built question makes every later step of evidence-based practice easier.

## What PICO stands for

- **P: Patient, population or problem.** Who are you asking about?
- **I: Intervention or exposure.** What treatment, test or factor are you interested in?
- **C: Comparison.** What is the alternative? Another treatment, placebo, usual care, or nothing.
- **O: Outcome.** What result matters? Mortality, pain, length of stay, accuracy.

Some versions add **T** for time (PICOT) or **S** for study design (PICOS).

## PICO question examples

### Therapy

*In adults with type 2 diabetes (P), does adding an SGLT2 inhibitor (I) compared with metformin alone (C) reduce hospital admission for heart failure (O)?*

### Prevention

*In older adults in care homes (P), does a structured exercise programme (I) compared with usual activity (C) reduce falls over 12 months (O, T)?*

### Diagnosis

*In children presenting with acute abdominal pain (P), how accurate is point-of-care ultrasound (I) compared with CT (C) for diagnosing appendicitis (O)?*

### Prognosis

*In patients after a first stroke (P), does early mobilisation within 24 hours (I) compared with later mobilisation (C) affect independence at three months (O)?*

### Nursing practice

*In post-operative adult patients (P), does chlorhexidine skin preparation (I) compared with povidone-iodine (C) reduce surgical site infection (O)?*

These examples show the structure. They are not clinical recommendations.

## How to write your own

1. **Start with the real problem** in plain words: "Does early mobilisation help after a stroke?"
2. **Fill each letter.** If you can't name a comparison, write "usual care" or "no intervention".
3. **Make the outcome measurable.** "Better recovery" becomes "independence at three months".
4. **Write it as one sentence.** If it needs two, split it into two questions.

## From PICO to search terms

Each PICO element becomes a block of search terms. Within a block, join synonyms with OR. Between blocks, join with AND. For example: (stroke OR cerebrovascular accident) AND (early mobilisation OR early mobilization) AND (functional independence OR activities of daily living).

Leave the comparison out of the search at first: it often narrows results too much.

## Common mistakes

- **Too broad:** "Is exercise good for older people?" has no clear outcome.
- **Too many questions at once:** one PICO per question.
- **Outcomes that can't be measured:** choose something studies actually report.
