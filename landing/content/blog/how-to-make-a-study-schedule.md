Most study schedules fail in the first week because they're built for an imaginary person with no fatigue, no surprises and unlimited motivation. A schedule that works is smaller than you think, and it plans for the days that go wrong.

## Step 1: List what you have to learn

Write every topic for every exam or module. Break big topics into pieces you could study in one sitting: "Renal physiology" becomes "nephron structure", "filtration", "acid-base", and so on.

## Step 2: Count your real hours

Look at a normal week and block out classes, work, travel, sleep and time with people. What's left is your study time. Be honest. If you have ten real hours, plan nine.

## Step 3: Block the time

Put study blocks in your calendar like appointments, 25 to 50 minutes each with breaks. Give each block one topic and one task: "Make flashcards for nephron structure", not "study renal".

## Step 4: Build in spaced review

New material needs revisiting: the next day, a few days later and a week later. Reserve 20 to 30 percent of your blocks for review, not new content.

## Step 5: Plan a weekly reset

Once a week, spend 20 minutes on:

- What did I finish?
- What did I get wrong in practice questions?
- What moves to next week?

Then adjust. A schedule that changes is a schedule that's working.

## A template you can copy

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | New topic A | Practice questions A | Review last week |
| Tuesday | New topic B | Practice questions B | Flashcards A |
| Wednesday | New topic C | Practice questions C | Flashcards B |
| Thursday | New topic D | Practice questions D | Mixed review |
| Friday | Mixed questions | Fix weak spots | Flashcards C and D |
| Saturday | Light review | | |
| Sunday | Rest | | |

## Tips that make it stick

- Start each block with the hardest task.
- Keep your phone in another room.
- Study with friends for accountability, but agree on silent focus time.
- Protect sleep. Memory consolidation happens overnight.
