AI has become part of how most students study. Used well, it saves hours. Used badly, it produces invented references, shallow understanding and academic misconduct cases. The difference is mostly in how you use it.

## Where AI helps

- **Finding your way into a topic:** explaining unfamiliar terms and concepts in plain language.
- **Reading faster:** summarising a paper you've already chosen, or answering specific questions about it.
- **Comparing sources:** laying out methods and findings side by side.
- **Practising:** turning notes into flashcards and practice questions.
- **First drafts of structure:** outlines and section plans you then rewrite.

## Where AI causes problems

- **Invented citations and facts:** general chatbots can produce references and statistics that don't exist.
- **Outdated information:** models are trained on data up to a cutoff.
- **Shallow learning:** letting AI do the thinking means you don't learn the material.
- **Academic integrity:** submitting AI-written work as your own breaks many institutions' rules.

## What to look for in an AI research tool

1. **Grounded answers.** It should answer from sources you choose, not just from memory.
2. **Named sources.** You should be able to see which paper an answer came from.
3. **Honesty about gaps.** It should say "this isn't in your material" rather than guess.
4. **Real search.** Papers should come from real databases, with links you can open.
5. **Full text.** Answers based only on abstracts miss methods and limitations.
6. **Privacy.** Check whether your content is used to train models.

## Rules for using AI honestly

- Check your institution's AI policy, and follow it.
- Use AI to understand and practise, not to write work you submit as your own.
- Verify every fact and citation against the original source.
- Be transparent when your course asks you to declare AI use.
