Hitting a paywall when you need one specific paper is frustrating, especially when a single article can cost more than a textbook. The good news is that a legal free copy often exists. Try these in order.

## 1. Look for an open access version

Many papers are published open access, or have a free version somewhere else. Search the exact title in Google Scholar and click "All versions", or in Semantic Scholar, and look for a PDF link.

## 2. Use Unpaywall

Unpaywall is a free service that looks up a DOI and finds a legal open copy, often an author's accepted manuscript in a university repository. There's a browser extension, and many tools use its database.

## 3. Check for a preprint

In physics, maths, computer science and increasingly biology and medicine, authors often post a preprint before or after publication: arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv and SSRN are common places. Check whether the final published version differs before you cite details.

## 4. Search PubMed Central and Europe PMC

Many biomedical papers funded by public bodies must be made free after a set period. PubMed Central and Europe PMC host millions of free full-text articles.

## 5. Check your library

If you're a student or staff member, your university library probably subscribes to more journals than you think. Sign in through the library's website, not directly on the publisher's site. Ask about interlibrary loan for anything they don't have.

## 6. Email the author

Researchers are usually happy to share their work. A short, polite email asking for a copy for your studies often gets a reply within days. Find their address on the paper or their university page.

## 7. Look in institutional repositories

Universities run repositories where staff deposit their papers. Search the author's institution plus the title.

## 8. Try open-access-only sources

DOAJ lists fully open journals, PLOS publishes everything open access, and Zenodo hosts papers, datasets and posters.

## What not to do

Sites that distribute paywalled papers without permission infringe copyright, and some carry security risks. The legal routes above cover most needs.
