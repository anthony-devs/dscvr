Cramming works for tomorrow and fails for next month. Spaced repetition is the opposite: reviewing material at increasing intervals so it moves into long-term memory. It's one of the most consistently supported findings in the science of learning.

## Why spacing works

When you review something just as you're starting to forget it, the effort of remembering strengthens the memory more than reviewing it while it's still fresh. Each successful review lets you wait longer before the next one. This idea goes back to Hermann Ebbinghaus's experiments on forgetting in the 1880s and has been studied extensively since.

## A simple schedule

You don't need software to start. A common pattern:

1. Learn it today.
2. Review tomorrow.
3. Review in three days.
4. Review in a week.
5. Review in two to three weeks.

If you get something wrong, start its schedule again from the short interval.

## Combine it with active recall

Spacing works best when each review is a test, not a reread. Answer the question from memory first, then check. Flashcards and practice questions are ideal for this.

## Interleave topics

Mixing related topics in one session, rather than finishing one completely before the next, helps you learn to tell similar ideas apart. It feels harder, which is part of why it works.

## Common mistakes

- **Reviewing only what you know.** Spend more time on cards you miss.
- **Cards that are too big.** One idea per card.
- **Starting too late.** Spacing needs weeks, not days.
- **Skipping reviews.** A missed review makes the next one harder, so keep sessions short enough to do daily.
