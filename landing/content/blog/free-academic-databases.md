Paywalls are the first wall most students hit. The second is not knowing where else to look. A huge amount of research is free to read, legally, if you know the right sources.

Here are ten worth bookmarking, and what each one is good for.

## 1. arXiv

A preprint server for physics, mathematics, computer science, statistics, quantitative biology, economics and related fields. Almost everything is free to read as a PDF. Papers are posted before peer review, so check whether a later published version exists.

**Best for:** machine learning, maths, physics, anything fast-moving.

## 2. PubMed and Europe PMC

PubMed indexes the biomedical literature. Europe PMC covers the same ground and more, adds full text for many open-access papers through PubMed Central, and includes preprints and clinical guidelines.

**Best for:** medicine, nursing, pharmacy and public health.

## 3. Semantic Scholar

A free, AI-assisted search engine from the Allen Institute for AI, covering most disciplines. Its strength is connections: citation counts, influential citations, and related papers.

**Best for:** finding the important papers in an area, and what came next.

## 4. OpenAlex

An open catalogue of scholarly works, authors, institutions and journals, built as a free replacement for paid indexes. It's huge and well structured.

**Best for:** broad searches across every discipline, including the humanities and social sciences.

## 5. Crossref

The registry behind most DOIs. Searching Crossref finds the official record for a published article, which is useful when you have a half-remembered title.

**Best for:** confirming that a paper exists and finding its DOI.

## 6. DOAJ

The Directory of Open Access Journals lists peer-reviewed journals that are free to read, and lets you search their articles.

**Best for:** fully open, peer-reviewed articles.

## 7. PLOS

The Public Library of Science publishes open-access journals, including PLOS ONE, PLOS Medicine and PLOS Biology. Everything is free to read and reuse.

**Best for:** biology and medicine, with full text always available.

## 8. Zenodo

A general-purpose open repository run by CERN. Researchers deposit papers, datasets, software and posters there.

**Best for:** datasets, code and conference material that isn't anywhere else.

## 9. Unpaywall

Not a search engine but a lookup: give it a DOI and it finds a legal, free copy if one exists, often an author's accepted manuscript in a university repository.

**Best for:** getting past a paywall legally.

## 10. Find Case Law (UK)

The National Archives' free service for judgments from courts and tribunals in England and Wales and the UK Supreme Court.

**Best for:** law students who need the text of a judgment.

## The problem: ten tabs

Each of these sources has its own search box, its own filters and its own quirks. Searching three or four of them for every question gets old quickly, and it's easy to miss the paper that only one of them indexes.

## Searching them together

DSCVR's Discover search runs one query across arXiv, Semantic Scholar, OpenAlex, Europe PMC, Crossref, DOAJ, PLOS and Zenodo at once. It merges duplicates, labels every result with its source, and can filter to papers with a free PDF. Health searches can switch to Europe PMC's clinical sources, and law searches can switch to UK case law. When a paper has a DOI but no PDF, "Find a free PDF" checks Unpaywall for a legal copy.

From there you can save papers to your library, read them in the built-in PDF reader, and ask Pinda, DSCVR's AI research assistant, questions that are answered from the papers you saved.
