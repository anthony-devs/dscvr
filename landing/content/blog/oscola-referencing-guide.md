OSCOLA (the Oxford University Standard for the Citation of Legal Authorities) is the referencing style most UK law schools use. It uses footnotes rather than in-text citations, and it has specific rules for cases and legislation. This guide covers the formats you'll use most. Always check your school's own guidance, which may vary slightly.

## The basics

- Citations go in **footnotes**, marked with a superscript number after the punctuation in the sentence.
- Footnotes end with a full stop.
- OSCOLA uses minimal punctuation: no full stops in abbreviations like "HL" or "QB".
- Many essays also need a **bibliography**, and some need a separate **table of cases** and **table of legislation**.

## Cases

The general form is: *Case name* [year] volume report first page (court).

> *Donoghue v Stevenson* [1932] AC 562 (HL).

- Case names are in italics, with "v" (no full stop).
- Square brackets are used when the year is needed to find the volume. Round brackets are used when the report series has its own volume numbers.
- Since 2001, many judgments have a **neutral citation**, which comes first: *R (Miller) v Secretary of State for Exiting the European Union* [2017] UKSC 5, [2018] AC 61.
- To point to a specific paragraph, add it at the end: [2017] UKSC 5 [44].

## Statutes

UK Acts are cited by short title and year, with no comma:

> Human Rights Act 1998.

To point to a section, add it after the year: Human Rights Act 1998, s 3. Use "ss" for several sections and "sch" for a schedule.

## Journal articles

> Author, 'Title of Article' (year) volume Journal Name first page.

> Jane Smith, 'Rethinking Duty of Care' (2020) 136 LQR 45.

(A made-up example to show the format.) Article titles go in single quotation marks. Journal names are not italicised; many are abbreviated.

## Books

> Author, *Title* (edition, Publisher year) page.

> Jane Smith, *Principles of Tort Law* (3rd edn, OUP 2021) 112.

(Again a made-up example, to show the format.)

## Websites and blogs

> Author, 'Title' (Website, date) URL accessed date.

Check whether your school still requires the "accessed" date; recent guidance has relaxed this in some places.

## Subsequent citations

After the first full citation, use a short form and point back to the first footnote:

> *Donoghue* (n 3) 580.

Use "ibid" for the same source as the immediately preceding footnote.

## Bibliography

In the bibliography, authors' surnames come first, then initials, with no pinpoint pages. Cases and legislation usually go in separate tables rather than the bibliography.
