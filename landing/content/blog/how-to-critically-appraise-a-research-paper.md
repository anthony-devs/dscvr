Critical appraisal means judging how much to trust a study and whether its results apply to your question. It's a core skill in medicine, nursing, public health and every research degree. Most appraisal frameworks come down to three questions.

## The three big questions

1. **Are the results valid?** Was the study designed and run in a way that minimises bias?
2. **What are the results?** How big is the effect, and how precise?
3. **Are the results relevant?** Do they apply to your population and setting?

## Checklist: validity

- Did the study ask a clearly focused question?
- Was the design right for that question?
- How were participants selected? Could selection bias explain the result?
- In a trial, was allocation randomised and concealed?
- Were participants, clinicians and assessors blinded where possible?
- Were groups similar at the start?
- How many people dropped out, and were they accounted for?
- Were outcomes measured the same way in all groups?
- Were confounders considered and adjusted for?

## Checklist: results

- What is the main result, and in what units?
- How large is the effect? A statistically significant result can still be too small to matter.
- How precise is it? Look at confidence intervals, not just p-values.
- Do the conclusions follow from the results?

## Checklist: relevance

- Are the participants similar to the people you care about?
- Were all important outcomes considered, including harms?
- Are the benefits worth the harms and costs?

## By study design

- **Randomised trials:** focus on randomisation, concealment, blinding and loss to follow-up.
- **Cohort studies:** focus on how exposure was measured, confounding, and completeness of follow-up.
- **Case-control studies:** focus on how cases and controls were selected and recall bias.
- **Qualitative studies:** focus on how participants were chosen, how data were collected and analysed, and the researchers' reflexivity.

Published checklists, such as those from CASP (the Critical Appraisal Skills Programme), give a structured set of questions for each design.

## Writing up your appraisal

Don't just list problems. Weigh them: say which flaws matter most and how they change your confidence in the conclusion.
