Nursing exams test more than memory. Most questions ask you to apply knowledge to a patient: what to assess first, which action is safest, what the result means. That's why rereading notes rarely works. Here's an approach that does.

## Understand what the exam rewards

Nursing exams, including licensing exams like the NCLEX in the United States, focus on **clinical judgment**: recognising cues, prioritising, choosing actions and evaluating outcomes. Since 2023 the NCLEX has included case-study style questions that follow one patient through several decisions. Even if you're not sitting the NCLEX, your course exams likely work the same way.

## 1. Learn the content in small blocks

Study one system or topic at a time: cardiac, respiratory, renal, endocrine, mental health, maternity, paediatrics, pharmacology. Short focused sessions beat long unfocused ones.

## 2. Test yourself straight away

After each block, close your notes and answer questions. Practice questions are where learning sticks, especially if you read the explanation for every answer, including the ones you got right.

## 3. Practise prioritisation

For any scenario, ask:

- **ABCs:** airway, breathing, circulation first.
- **Acute before chronic, unstable before stable.**
- **Safety first:** what prevents harm right now?
- **Assess before you act**, unless the patient is in immediate danger.

## 4. Master pharmacology with flashcards

For each drug class, know the mechanism in a line, key uses, major side effects, important nursing considerations and what to monitor. Flashcards are perfect for this. One drug class per card set.

## 5. Space your review

Review new material the next day, again after three days, then a week later. Spend more time on what you got wrong.

## A weekly plan

- **Monday to Thursday:** one topic a day, learn in the morning, 20 practice questions in the evening.
- **Friday:** mixed questions across the week's topics.
- **Saturday:** review everything you got wrong this week.
- **Sunday:** rest, or a light flashcard review.
